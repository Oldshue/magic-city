## 1. THE DIVERGENCE

### The Moment: November 1907 — TCI Refuses the Swap

In the real record, U.S. Steel acquired Tennessee Coal, Iron & Railroad (TCI) in November 1907, during the Panic of 1907, absorbing the one company whose mills could undersell Pittsburgh anywhere east of the Rockies. Judge Elbert H. Gary then extended **Pittsburgh Plus** pricing to TCI's output: steel made beside its own coal, ore, and limestone was priced as if hauled 1,000 miles from Pennsylvania. The FTC finally ordered Pittsburgh Plus abolished in 1924 — and even then a ~$4/ton "Birmingham differential" survived until Alabama protested it in 1934. Birmingham's population still grew from 132,685 (1910) to 259,678 (1930) *under* that handicap.

**Divergence:** In this timeline, on **November 4, 1907**, TCI president **Joseph H. Woodward** refuses the U.S. Steel bond exchange. Moore & Schley is rescued instead by a syndicate led by New Orleans cotton-factors and Baltimore rail money — capital hungry for a southern industrial champion. TCI stays independent, keeps its cost advantage, and never accepts phantom freight.

Consequence logic: no Pittsburgh Plus means every dollar of freight saved becomes margin or expansion. Birmingham underprices Pittsburgh across the whole South, Mexico, Cuba, and Latin American export markets. Capital floods Jones Valley. By 1929 it is the steel city of the South at roughly Chicago-of-the-South scale.

## 2. THE CITY IN 1929

**Population:** 640,000 city proper; 1,050,000 in Jefferson County metro. Growth has been continuous since 1907; the city absorbed Ensley, Avondale, Woodlawn, Fairfield, and Bessemer into a single transit-connected conurbation.

**Industry:** Annual steel output **14.2 million tons** — pig iron 6.8M tons, finished steel 7.4M. Employers of record:
- **Tennessee Coal, Iron & Railroad Co. (TCI)** — independent since 1907; HQ in the 31-story TCI Building. Works at Ensley, Fairfield, and Bessemer.
- **Sloss Furnaces Co.** — pig iron and the famous Sloss No. 2 iron that cast Vulcan; glowing furnace district north of downtown.
- **Woodward Iron Co., Republic Steel Southern Division, Gulf States Steel** — secondary works along the valley floor.
- Supporting trades: **Birmingham Stove & Range**, cast-pipe foundries, rail-car shops, and the **Southern Ore & Limestone Exchange** (invented commodities board).

The geology is unchanged and real: coal, hematite ore, and limestone all within a few miles of the furnaces — the only place on earth where that is true. That fact is the city's civic religion.

**Wealth & power:** Per-capita bank deposits are the highest in the South. Who runs it:
- **Mayor Aloysius P. Crandall** (invented), "the Smelter Mayor," elected 1927 on a paving-and-power platform backed by TCI and the streetcar interests.
- The **Jefferson Trust** (banking house of the Hand family heirs) finances downtown construction.
- **Colonel Ransom Beekman DeLancey**, TCI chairman, is the closest thing to a baron; his mansion crowns Highland Avenue.
- Labor: 90,000 organized steelworkers; the **Valley Federation of Labor** holds real power after the 1921 Accord ended the strike era with a wage-and-hours compact unique in heavy industry.

**Walking the city:** You come out of Terminal Station under a tiled Byzantine dome, cross a streetcar tunnel plaza, and look up a canyon of 20th Street North toward Red Mountain, five miles south, where a 56-foot iron Vulcan stands on a 120-foot tower, his spear lit by red neon at dusk. The air smells of coal smoke and cut limestone. Fourteen streetcar lines clatter below the elevated loop. Sidewalks are marble-thresholded; banks compete in lobby height. It is loud, sooty, confident — a steel town that won.

## 3. DISTRICTS (walkable zones for the open world)

### 1. THE HEAVIEST CORNER (downtown core)
Grown outward from the real 20th St & 1st Ave N cluster into a full art-deco canyon of towers 20–35 stories. Narrowest skyscraper density in the South.
**Signature:** First National–Hand Tower (21 st, real); Brown-Marx Building (16 st, real); Empire Building (16 st, real); Woodward Building (10 st, real); **Jefferson Trust Tower** (34 st, invented, ziggurat crown).
**Sounds:** streetcars below grade, bank chimes, ticker tape, shoe leather on terrazzo.
**Street life:** bankers, bond men, secretaries at lunch counters, newsboys shouting *Ledger* extras.

### 2. FURNACE ROW / SLOSS FLATS (industrial north)
North of downtown along the valley floor: Sloss Furnaces, pipe foundries, casting sheds. At night the sky over the district is a low orange dome; slag pours glow like slow lava.
**Signature:** Sloss Furnaces complex (real, incl. 1927 charging tunnels); **Sloss Powerhouse & Blowing Engines Hall**; **Slag Glass Works** (invented — makes amber glassware sold nationwide); **Furnacemen's Union Hall No. 3**.
**Sounds:** blowing engines' heartbeat, whistle shifts, crane bells, hiss of quench.
**Street life:** shift workers, lunch pails, company doctors, bootleggers at the third-shift door.

### 3. TERMINAL QUARTER (rail gateway)
Around the monumental 1909 Terminal Station and its six railroads; hotels, ticket agencies, the freight exchange.
**Signature:** Terminal Station with twin 130-foot towers and 64-foot dome (real); **Hotel Tutwiler Grand** (grown from the real Tutwiler); **Southern Railway Annex**; **WELCOME TO BIRMINGHAM sign** (real sign, amended text); **Union Depot Yards footbridge**.
**Sounds:** steam whistles on quarter-hours, porters' calls, taxi horns, the Wurlitzer drifting from the Alabama two blocks east.
**Street life:** travelers, Pullman conductors, flower sellers, bootblacks, jazz musicians stepping off the Cincinnati flyer.

### 4. RED MOUNTAIN CREST & VULCAN PARK
Five miles south, the mountain ridge that gave the city its ore. Cable funicular plus parkway roads reach the summit.
**Signature:** **Vulcan statue, 56 ft of Sloss pig iron on a 120-foot tower** (real statue; timeline-early placement per divergence); **Sentinel Observation Deck** (invented); **Ore Mines Overlook** (real mines, interpretive walk); **DeLancey Mansion** on Highland Ave below.
**Sounds:** wind, cable car gears, the city hum below.
**Street life:** Sunday families, honeymooners, painters, kite flyers.

### 5. THE ALABAMA WAY (theatre & radio district)
Second Avenue North between 19th and 22nd — movie palaces, vaudeville, radio studios, nightclubs.
**Signature:** **Alabama Theatre** (real, opened Dec 26, 1927); **Ritz Vaudeville House** (invented); **WBRC & WAPI studio block** (real call letters, invented building); **Club Savoy Ballroom-South** (invented);
**Sounds:** marquee bulbs buzzing, Wurlitzer intermission music, big-band rehearsal through propped doors, radio drama applause.
**Street life:** matinee crowds, autograph seekers, cigarette girls, band boys carrying horns.

### 6. AVONDALE & THE BOULEVARD (residential middle)
Streetcar-suburb belt of bungalows and merchant homes around Avondale Park; where foremen, shopkeepers, and the new union aristocracy live.
**Signature:** **Avondale Park & Spring** (real); **Avondale Mills Office** (real firm, grown campus); **Valley Federation of Labor Temple** (invented); **Birmingham Public Library East Branch** (invented Carnegie-style).
**Sounds:** lawnmowers, porch radios (KDKA, WBRC), church bells, kids playing under elms.

### 7. ENSLEY WORKS & MERCATO (southwest industrial-port quarter)
The great TCI South Works and the immigrant quarter that grew around it — Italian, Greek, Polish, Black neighborhoods side by side; the city's loudest, most aromatic streets.
**Signature:** **Ensley Blast Furnace Row** (real site, expanded); **Mercato Public Market** (invented); **St. Elias Greek Orthodox Church** (invented); **Ensley Workers' Institute** (night school, invented).
**Sounds:** furnace shift sirens, market vendors in four languages, accordion and bottleneck guitar from open windows.

## 5. TEXTURE (use consistently everywhere)

**Signage & slogans**
- "WELCOME TO BIRMINGHAM — STEEL CAPITAL OF THE SOUTH"
- "THE HEAVIEST CORNER ON EARTH" — etched over the corner bank entrances
- TCI slogan: "MADE WHERE IT'S MINED — TC IRON"
- Sloss: "SLOSS — THE IRON THAT BUILT THE SOUTH SINCE 1882"
- Ledger masthead motto: "THE VOICE OF THE VALLEY"
- Vulcan Park plaque: "CAST OF SLOSS NO. 2 PIG IRON, 1904 — SENTINEL OF THE MAGIC CITY"

**Streetcars** — operated by **Birmingham Electric Co.** (this timeline keeps that name from 1921):
| Line | Color / rollsign | Route |
|------|------------------|-------|
| Red Line | red | Terminal Station ↔ Red Mountain funicular |
| Ensley Flyer | orange | Downtown ↔ Ensley Works & Mercato |
| Avondale Local | green | 20th Street ↔ Avondale Park |
| Belt Loop (elevated) | white/gold | Elevated circuit of the core |
| Bessemer Limited | blue | Downtown ↔ Bessemer mills |
| Woodlawn Crosstown | brown | East-west suburban crosstown |
Fare: **5¢** (7¢ with transfer). Cars are Pay-As-You-Enter Birneys on local lines, big center-door cars on the Limiteds.

**Newspapers:** *The Birmingham Ledger* (morning, Voice of the Valley), *The Age-Herald* (evening), *The Valley Worker* (labor weekly), *The Magic City Messenger* (Black weekly, 4th Avenue).

**Radio:** **WBRC** 1140 kc (real call letters; "The Voice of Vulcan"), **WAPI** 1190 kc (studio block on Alabama Way), and **WSGN** 1310 kc (invented; race results and hillbilly hour). KDKA and WSB Atlanta come in clearly on porch sets.

**Music:** Territory big bands at Club Savoy and the Tutwiler roof; Wurlitzer organ at the Alabama (intermission sing-alongs); sacred harp singings in Avondale; blues and bottleneck in the Mercato; the **Magic City Symphony** at the new Municipal Auditorium.

**Cars:** Model A Fords everywhere; Chevrolets; Packards and Cadillacs for the Corner set; TCI company trucks; Yellow Cab fleet off Terminal Plaza. Traffic signals just installed downtown — cops still direct the Corner at rush hours.

**Prices of everyday things (canon):**
- Loaf of bread 7¢ · coffee 15¢ cup at a lunch counter · Coca-Cola 5¢
- Streetcar fare 5¢ · movie matinee 25¢, evening 50¢ (balcony 35¢)
- Men's haircut 25¢ · shoeshine 5¢ · pack of Lucky Strikes 10¢
- Steelworker's wage $6.20/day under the 1921 Accord (above the national norm — the Accord's pride)
- Three-room flat in Avondale $32/mo · room at the Tutwiler $3.50/night
- New Model A Ford $485 · used Chevy $95
