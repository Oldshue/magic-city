# MAGIC CITY 1929 — WORLD BIBLE
*Single source of truth for all builders. Alternate-history Birmingham, Alabama, autumn 1929. Real record before the divergence; extrapolation after.*

---

## 1. THE DIVERGENCE

**The moment: July 21, 1924.** In our timeline the FTC ordered U.S. Steel to abandon "Pittsburgh Plus" basing-point pricing — steel made in Birmingham was priced as Pittsburgh mill price plus phantom freight from Pittsburgh to the buyer, erasing Birmingham's natural advantage (coal, ore, and limestone within a few miles of each other). Even after 1924, multiple-basing-point schemes kept a punitive "Birmingham differential" (the southern pig-iron differential survived into the late 1930s).

**Divergence:** On July 21, 1924, U.S. Steel does not merely comply with the FTC order — it *concedes the basing point*. Judge Elbert H. Gary's successor board, facing the FTC order, an antitrust-scarred corporation, and rising southern demand, adopts **FOB-the-mill pricing nationwide**. Every ton is priced from where it is actually poured. Overnight, Birmingham steel undercuts every northern mill east of the Mississippi on delivered price.

**Why this moment:** It is the single documented decision point where the suppression mechanism was legally exposed and could have collapsed entirely instead of being replaced by another differential system. One corporate signature, not a fantasy.

## 2. THE CITY IN 1929

- **Population:** 512,000 (city), 830,000 (Jefferson County metro). Second-largest city in the South by population after New Orleans; first by industrial payroll.
- **Industry:** 6.4 million tons of steel annually across TCI Ensley, Sloss, Woodward, and Republic-Birmingham. Coke ovens at Thomas and Docena; ore mines on Red Mountain and in Shades Valley; limestone quarries at Gate City. Birmingport moves 2 million tons/yr by barge.
- **Wealth:** Per-capita bank deposits triple the real 1929 figure; four national banks, led by **First National–Hand Trust**. A genuine (if small) black business district thrives along Fourth Avenue North — the "Black Main Street" — anchored by the Penny Savings Bank and the Masonic Temple.
- **Who runs it:** The **Iron Coalition** — TCI president **Aldrich Vance-Holt** (invented), Mayor **Buford Callahan** (invented, third term, machine-backed), the *Birmingham Ledger* publisher **Hallie Whitcomb Deas** (invented), and the Chamber of Commerce's permanent chairman **Roscoe P. Tutwiler** (invented). Labor is organized but tense: Amalgamated lodges at Ensley, company unions elsewhere.
- **How it feels:** Jones Valley holds smoke like a bowl holds water. At dawn the valley is gray-blue; by noon the sun burns through amber; at night the furnace glow paints Red Mountain's crest orange. Downtown is a canyon of brick and terra cotta along 20th Street, streetcars clanging through, coal soot on every white collar. It smells of coke, creosote, and magnolia. It is loud, proud, self-conscious about its nickname, and utterly certain the twentieth century belongs to it.
- **Hard canon numbers:** 512,000 pop.; 6.4M tons steel; 9 buildings over 20 stories; 41 streetcar routes; 3 daily newspapers; 4 radio stations; 22 hotels over 10 stories; Terminal Station handles 84 passenger trains/day.

## 3. DISTRICTS

### A. The Corner (Downtown Canyon)
First Avenue North & 20th Street — the Heaviest Corner grown vertical. Character: financial, brisk, marble lobbies, hat-brim crowd.
- Woodward Building (grown to 24 floors)
- Brown-Marx Building
- **Jefferson Tower** (invented, 34 floors, art-deco setbacks, tallest in the South)
- First National–Hand Building & annex
- **Basing Point Arcade** (invented covered shopping arcade named for the victory)
*Sounds:* streetcar gongs, ticker tape, shoe heels on terrazzo. *Street:* bankers, stenographers, bond men, newsboys shouting Ledger extras.

### B. Furnace Row (Sloss–East Thomas)
The industrial heart, glowing all night. Character: firelight, rail spurs, shift-change rivers of men.
- Sloss Furnaces Nos. 1–5 (rebuilt 1927)
- **The Five Stack Line** (invented row of chimneys visible for 15 miles)
- Sloss City Furnaces office (real)
- **Cinderline YMCA** (invented)
- East Thomas coke yards
*Sounds:* furnace roar, casting-floor bells, locomotive couplings. *Street:* puddlers, roustabouts, lunch pails, mule teams giving way to Mack trucks.

### C. Terminal Quarter
Around the 1909 Terminal Station and its ten tracks.
- Terminal Station (dome, twin 130-ft towers) — expanded 1928 with the **Concourse Rotunda**
- **Hotel Alagasco** (invented, 16 floors, grand lobby)
- Morris Avenue wholesale row (real)
- **Union Freight House** (invented)
- **DeBardeleben Annex** (invented rail-office tower)
*Sounds:* steam whistles on the quarter-hour, baggage carts, train-call echoes. *Street:* travelers, Pullman porters, flower sellers, bootblacks.

### D. Red Crest (Vulcan Park & Highlands)
Red Mountain's summit above the city.
- Vulcan, 56 feet of iron atop his pedestal (relocated here permanently in this timeline, 1925, in a civic triumph)
- **Vulcan Observation Colonnade** (invented)
- Highland Avenue boulevard mansions
- **Redmont Grand Hotel** (grown from the real Redmont)
- **Ore Bank Overlook** (invented terrace over the old mines)
*Sounds:* wind in pines, distant mill hum, band concerts Sundays. *Street:* promenading families, courting couples, tourists with Kodaks.

### E. The Bijou Strip (Third Avenue Theater & Radio District)
Third and Fourth Avenues North between 17th and 21st.
- Alabama Theatre (1817 Third Ave N, "Showplace of the South")
- **Ritz-Variety Hall** (invented vaudeville house)
- **WJLN Broadcast Studio** (invented, glass-fronted studio above a pharmacy)
- **Bijou Roof Garden** (invented dance hall)
- Lyric Theatre (real, kept)
*Sounds:* Wurlitzer organ leaking onto the sidewalk, marquee bulbs, jazz from doorways. *Street:* flappers, matinee lines, sheet-music hawkers.

### F. Fourth Avenue (The Black Main Street)
Center of Black commerce and culture.
- **Penny Savings Bank tower annex** (grown)
- **Masonic Temple** (real, kept, 1922)
- **Carver Colored Auditorium** (invented, 2,000 seats)
- **Eclipse Records & Phonographs** (invented shop)
- **Golden Rule Cafeteria** (invented)
*Sounds:* blues piano from upstairs rooms, church bells, political argument. *Street:* teachers, barbers, lodge brothers, gospel quartets.

### G. Birmingport Docks (Black Warrior Riverfront, Southwest)
Opened 1928.
- **Inland Exchange Elevator & Warehouse** (invented)
- **Port Authority Crane Wharf No. 2** (invented)
- **Dockworkers' Hall** (invented union hall)
- River barge fleet moorings
- **Tutwiler Yacht Basin** (invented, small, much mocked)
*Sounds:* crane chains, river foghorns, cargo chants. *Street:* stevedores, river rats, speculators reading tonnage boards.

## 5. TEXTURE

- **Slogans/signage:** "THE SOUTH'S IRON THRONE"; "PRICED WHERE IT'S POURED"; "MAGIC CITY — MADE OF STEEL, RUN ON STEAM"; Dr Pepper "10-2-4"; Coca-Cola; "Smoke Signals — Buy War... er, Buy Bonds." Cigar-store Indians, painted-wall Mail Pouch ads.
- **Newspapers:** *Birmingham Ledger* (morning, Deas family), *Iron Age Dispatch* (evening, labor-leaning), *Magic City Herald* (sensational evening).
- **Radio:** WJLN (Bijou Strip, 1210 kc), WBRC kept (real callsign, 1925), WKBC (Furnace Row, mill bulletins), WAIV (Terminal Qtr). Call letters painted on rooftops.
- **Streetcars:** Birmingham Electric Co. — 41 routes; named lines with painted destination rolls: **RED MOUNTAIN LINE** (green roll), **ENSLEY WORKS EXPRESS** (red), **FAIRFIELD LOOP** (amber), **HIGHLAND AVENUE** (cream), **DOCKS LINE** (blue), **AVONDALE** (orange). Fare 6¢, tokens 5 for 25¢.
- **Music:** Birmingham-born jazz and blues on Fourth Avenue; Wurlitzer organ at the Alabama; W.C. Handy's memory invoked; hillbilly acts at the Ritz; Sunday brass at Vulcan Park.
- **Cars:** Model A Fords ($495 roadster), Packards for the Corner set ($2,200), Studebaker taxis, Mack trucks hauling ingots.
- **Prices:** cup of coffee 5¢; plate lunch 25¢; movie ticket 30¢; shave 15¢; room at Hotel Alagasco $3.50; streetcar 6¢; pound of bacon 28¢; men's felt hat $5.
- **Weather/light canon:** valley haze; furnace glow visible citywide after dusk; soot on white shirts by 3 pm.
